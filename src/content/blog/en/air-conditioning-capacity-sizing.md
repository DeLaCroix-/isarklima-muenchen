Correct cooling capacity is not simply the largest number available on a product label. An air conditioning system should remove the heat entering a room under the intended design conditions without being unnecessarily large. Too little capacity can mean continuous full-load operation and inadequate temperatures on hot days. Substantial oversizing can lead to short cycling, reduced comfort or dehumidification and avoidable cost.

## Factors that influence the cooling load

Cooling load is the rate of heat that must be removed from a space under defined conditions. It combines external and internal heat gains. For an initial review, a floor plan, ceiling height, window areas, orientation, shading, use and photographs are often sufficient. A reliable design may require additional building information.

The Bavarian consumer advice centre emphasises that sound planning and appropriate equipment selection help avoid unnecessarily high operating costs. Its current guidance is available under [Installing air conditioning: what should be considered?](https://www.verbraucherzentrale.bayern/wissen/energie/energetische-sanierung/klimaanlage-installieren-was-ist-zu-beachten-10525).

### Room size, glazing and solar gain

Floor area and room height define the air volume but explain only part of the load. Large windows, roof surfaces and unshaded south- or west-facing glazing can add substantial solar heat. External blinds work differently from an internal curtain because they stop more radiation before it enters the room.

Construction and insulation also matter. A heavy older building may store and release heat differently from a lightweight attic conversion. Open plans, stairwells and frequently opened doors alter the effective zone. The area to be cooled should therefore not automatically be treated as the entire dwelling.

### Occupants, equipment and operating hours

People release heat and moisture. A bedroom used by two people has a different profile from an occasional guest room. Offices add monitors, computers, lighting and other equipment. Kitchens or spaces with high-power technical equipment need separate consideration.

Operating periods matter as well. A room cooled only in the evening has a different load and control profile from an office operating throughout the day. The target temperature, open or closed windows and rooms used simultaneously all influence selection.

## Why a simple watts-per-square-metre rule is not enough

Generic watts-per-square-metre figures can support a rough screening, but they hide the difference between a shaded north-facing room and a highly glazed attic. They often ignore ceiling height, window quality, orientation, shading, occupants and equipment. A single internet value should not lead directly to equipment ordering.

The nominal capacity of an indoor unit should not be viewed in isolation either. In multi-split systems, available output depends on the outdoor unit, the permitted indoor-unit combination and simultaneous operation. Manufacturer documentation defines valid combinations and capacity ranges.

A larger system is not automatically the safer choice. The aim is an appropriate system with useful part-load capability. Alongside peak load, air distribution, noise, available space, pipe routes, electrical provision and behaviour during typical Munich summer conditions all matter.

## Data that helps with sizing

For each room, prepare length, width and height; window dimensions and orientation; type of shading; roof and external wall surfaces; building age or known renovation level; normal number of occupants; relevant equipment; operating periods and target temperature. A floor plan and clear photographs provide context.

For a house or several rooms, indicate which zones are expected to cool at the same time. This simultaneity is especially important for multi-split selection. Possible indoor-unit positions are useful too, because poor air distribution cannot be corrected simply by adding nominal capacity.

The calculation is only one part of design. The chosen system must also match pipe limits, outside-temperature range, acoustic requirements and current refrigerant rules. EU Regulation 2024/573 introduces phased provisions for certain split systems, so a product should be checked against its current market status and technical data. See [Regulation (EU) 2024/573](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/573/oj?locale=en).

## FAQs

### Can capacity be calculated from floor area alone?

No. Area is a starting point, but glazing, sunlight, ceiling height, construction, occupants, equipment and use can materially change the result.

### Is a larger air conditioner always better?

No. Oversizing can produce poor operating behaviour. The system should suit both the design load and normal part-load operation.

### Does every room need a separate assessment?

Yes where rooms have different orientation, use or heat gains. This is particularly important for separately controlled indoor units.

### Which photographs are useful?

Images of the proposed internal wall, windows and shading, outdoor location and intended pipe route support an initial review.
