The outdoor unit of a split system rejects heat outside. Its fan and compressor generate sound, while mounting points can transfer vibration into the building. Disturbance cannot be determined from one product-data decibel figure. Operation, installation, surroundings and distance to the receiver must be considered together.

## Sound power is not the same as noise at the receiver

Sound power describes the total acoustic energy emitted by a source. The sound pressure or immission at a receiver describes what arrives at a particular place. Distance, screening, reflections, directivity and other noise sources lie between the two. An outdoor unit with an apparently low catalogue figure can still be poorly positioned; careful siting can materially improve the result.

Germany’s TA Lärm governs the assessment of commercial and many technical noise sources. It distinguishes limits by area type and time. It lists 55 dB(A) by day and 40 at night for general residential areas, and 50 and 35 for purely residential areas. The receiving point matters; there is no universal limit beside the unit. The official rules are in [TA Lärm](https://www.verwaltungsvorschriften-im-internet.de/bsvwvbund_26081998_IG19980826.htm).

### Measurement point and area type

A robust assessment needs the applicable planning-area classification and the location of the nearest noise-sensitive window or other relevant receiver. A rough distance estimate is not sufficient in every case. Existing noise from other technical equipment may also be relevant.

Germany's Environment Agency explains that TA Lärm uses immission guide values and assessment criteria, and that the relevant receiver may be a protected room. Its overview of [industrial and commercial noise](https://www.umweltbundesamt.de/themen/laerm/nachbarschaftslaerm-laerm-von-anlagen/industrie-gewerbelaerm) also shows why sound power, propagation path and operating times need to be considered together.

### Reflections and night-time operation

Walls, parapets and narrow courtyards can reflect sound. Placing a unit in a corner is not automatically quieter even if it is less visible. The unit also needs unrestricted intake and discharge airflow; a thermally poor location can lead to longer or more intensive operating periods.

Night limits are normally stricter. A selectable silent or night mode may form part of the concept, but it does not replace checking its actual sound data and available cooling capacity. Start-up behaviour, defrost cycles during heating operation and short-duration peaks should also be considered where relevant.

## What makes a suitable location

A suitable location combines four requirements: free airflow, structurally secure and vibration-conscious mounting, access for installation and later work, and adequate protection for neighbours. Ownership and appearance are additional constraints. A technically attractive position cannot be used on a facade or common property without the required consent.

The review should also address condensate, rain, snow, dirt and accidental damage. An enclosure must not reduce manufacturer clearances or obstruct the required air volume.

### Distances and unrestricted airflow

Manufacturers specify minimum clearances at the intake, discharge and service sides. These values are product-specific and should not be replaced by generic internet figures. Heated discharge air should not immediately recirculate to the unit or be directed continuously towards neighbouring areas.

Greater distance from noise-sensitive windows is generally helpful, but it must remain compatible with permitted pipe lengths, access and the approved position. Screens can be effective in some geometries, yet require proper design so that airflow and maintenance are not compromised.

### Vibration isolation and mounting

Resilient mounts or suitable vibration isolators reduce transmission into brackets, balcony slabs and other building elements. They must match the unit’s weight and relevant frequencies. A soft pad without structural design is not a substitute for secure mounting.

Wall brackets can introduce structure-borne sound into the facade; floor-mounted units can transmit through roofs, terraces or balcony slabs. Neither arrangement is inherently superior. The building construction, bracket, isolators and workmanship determine the result. Load capacity, corrosion protection and fall safety remain important independently of acoustics.

## Checks before installation

Before a location is fixed, document equipment sound data, site plan, distances, area type, neighbouring windows, reflective surfaces, mounting build-up and intended operating periods. An acoustic calculation or specialist assessment may be necessary where the margin is unclear. Only after this review should the position be included in a landlord, owners’ association or authority application.

## FAQs

### Must every outdoor unit be below 35 dB(A) at night?

No. A single dB(A) value is not a universal equipment limit across Germany. Area type, receiver location, assessment period and the applicable evaluation method matter.

### Is silent mode sufficient evidence of compliance?

No. It may reduce sound output, but it must be assessed using reliable manufacturer data, the actual location and the required operating capacity.

### Is wall mounting noisier than floor mounting?

Not necessarily. Both can transmit vibration. The building construction, bracket or base, isolators and installation quality determine the outcome.

### Can a sound enclosure always be added later?

An enclosure must be compatible with the unit and must not improperly restrict airflow, performance or maintenance. It is not a universal remedy.
