An owner who wants split air conditioning in a condominium is making a decision about comfort inside the private unit, but the installation often affects parts of the building that are not owned individually. An outdoor unit, a wall penetration, visible pipework or a facade bracket may involve common property. The owners’ association process should therefore come before equipment ordering and detailed installation planning.

## When common property may be affected

Section 20 of the German Condominium Act governs structural alterations and their approval by resolution or permission from the community. Air conditioning is not listed there as one of the privileged measures. Whether a specific proposal alters common property or affects other owners must be assessed against the building, declaration of division and proposed installation. The official wording is available in [section 20 WEG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/woeigg/__20.html).

An owner should first consult the property manager and the relevant property documents. A technically plausible proposal helps the discussion but does not replace the necessary resolution. Equally, a resolution should not be so vague that location, construction and protective conditions remain undefined.

### Facade, balcony and wall penetration

An external wall is normally part of the building envelope. Even if the outdoor unit stands on a balcony, wall penetrations, connecting lines, railing attachments, external appearance and noise may affect shared interests. “It will stand on my balcony” is therefore not enough to conclude that no approval is needed.

The application should identify the exact location, clearances, mounting method, pipe route, penetration through the envelope and condensate discharge. If installation requires access through communal space, that practical requirement should be included as well.

### Resolution before installation

The resolution should be obtained before drilling, mounting or running pipes. Ideally, it refers to the submitted plans and records any conditions, such as vibration isolation, the colour of external pipe covers, maintenance access or reinstatement after later removal.

Approval by the owners does not replace a public-law assessment. Article 57 of the Bavarian Building Code treats many technical building installations as procedure-free, but the City of Munich stresses that all other public-law requirements continue to apply. See its official guide to [building without a normal permit procedure](https://stadt.muenchen.de/infos/verfahrensfreie-bauvorhaben.html).

## What documents the owners’ association needs to decide

The property manager and other owners should be able to understand what will be visible, what effects may arise and how they will be controlled. A useful submission contains a floor plan, facade or balcony photographs, a marked outdoor-unit position, the proposed pipe route and technical information for the intended system.

A listed building, protected ensemble or location near a monument may require separate permission. The City of Munich explains the scope and required documents on its page for [monument protection permission](https://stadt.muenchen.de/service/info/untere-denkmalschutzbehorde/1073937/).

### System, location and pipe route

The data sheet should at least show dimensions, operating range and outdoor sound power information. A drawing should show how the indoor and outdoor units are connected and where a pipe cover remains visible. The proposed core hole, sealing and any relevant fire-safety requirements must be assessed by qualified participants.

The layout should not be selected solely by choosing the shortest route. Air distribution, maintenance access, structural capacity, neighbouring windows and reliable condensate drainage all interact.

### Noise, vibration and condensate

Noise is assessed at the relevant receiving point. Germany’s TA Lärm distinguishes between area types and day and night periods, so a low catalogue value at the unit is only one element. The official limits are published in [TA Lärm](https://www.verwaltungsvorschriften-im-internet.de/bsvwvbund_26081998_IG19980826.htm).

The submission should also describe vibration isolators, nearby reflecting surfaces and intended night operation. Condensate must not drain uncontrolled over the facade or onto another unit. If a pump is needed, it creates additional noise and maintenance considerations.

## Technical planning only after approval

Before the resolution, a robust concept drawing is normally sufficient. Final capacity selection, detailed pipe routing and installation preparation should follow only when the approved version is clear. If the technical review changes a material part of the proposal, it must be checked whether the resolution needs to be amended.

## FAQs

### Does the owners’ association always have to approve air conditioning?

That depends on the ownership boundaries and method of installation. Where the facade, building envelope, external appearance or other shared interests may be affected, the required resolution should be clarified first.

### Is a resolution without a technical attachment sufficient?

A very general resolution can leave later disputes about its meaning. Drawings, system information and protective measures make the permitted version easier to identify.

### Does procedure-free also mean consent-free?

No. Building procedure, private-law consent and other public-law requirements are separate questions.

### Who assesses noise?

Designers and qualified contractors can provide technical data. Whether a formal calculation or authority review is necessary depends on the site and project.
